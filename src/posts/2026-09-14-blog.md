# Looking Forward

Some of the common reading requirements for undergraduate and graduate research ethics courses are mentioned in the Leiden Declaration website and include The Uppsala Code of Ethics for Scientists, the San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment, the UNESCO Recommendation on Open Science, and the UK Universal Ethical Code for Scientists. These are traditionally thought of as requirements to form a basis for understanding how to do research ethically and responsibly, in addition to online quizz based certifications that are required by universites for all researchers, such as the [Collaborative Institutional training Initiative](https://www.citiprogram.org/index.cfm?pageID=14) which provides training for hujman subject experiment researchers. It might be useful to think about the Leiden Declaration and other recent comments on the ethics of AI in research as required reading for future researchers. 

## Commonly Raised Concerns

The idea of requiring that students learn about AI ethics and tool use is likely problematic to many, but I think there are good reasons for its inclusion. 

1. I am not going to use AI in my research, so I don't think I need to learn about AI research ethics. 

While I understand the position behind wanting to totally cut off research methods from the use of AI by researchers who are in areas that are less directly related to it, I think that this position is overly optimistic about the behavior of other researchers. I personally have reviewd and served as program chair for conferences and editor of journals that have recieved a large number of seemingly AI written papers that required desk-rejects or recieved low scores from reviewers. However, as the advancement of AI in research writing continues, it is likely that our ability as researchers to detect AI written work will deteriorate. Better understanding the functioning of AI systems and how they are used in research will make us better able to determine if it is being misused in research contexts. 

2. I have AI copy-edit all my papers and it is great, forcing students to learn about past issues in AI will lead to slowed research progress. 

The polar oposite view of the first point is the idea that limiting the use of AI by others will unnecessarily hinder their ability to produce new research, and have an overall negative impact on the field. 

3. AI taking over research is inevitible, so humans won't be making these types of ethical decisions in the future that makes this education relevant. 

Along a similar line of the previous point is the view that even if AI isn't fully ingrained in all aspects of research now, it will be in the future. 

4. The use of AI itself is morally wrong, and education about its use in AI research tacitly approves of an immoral act.
