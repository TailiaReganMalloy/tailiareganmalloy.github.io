## Efficient LLM Moderation with Multi-Layer Latent Prototypes 

https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.16174

Alignment isn't enough at deployment, since robust moderation is required to make sure that LLMs behave correctly. A lot of the research into alignment shows that it is highly superficial often losing effectiveness after only a few generated tokens (e.g changing the tense of dangerous questions to past-tense can enable AI to reply). These are also evolving threats where not only are there jailbreak techniques but also novel attacks. 

This paper focuses on input moderation, a proactive defense that assesses the prompt before generation begins and refuses malicious prompts early, saving significant compute resources, without interfering with the model's internal reasoning process. 
- Question from me: What is the reason for not interfering with the model's internal reasoning process during input moderation? Or is that just an example of output moderation (i.e prompting the model to think about what it has said, and what it is reasoning about).

Guard Models are speciailized LLMs that have evolving inputs, like llamaguard, which has high performance but also high costs and rigidity, hard to customize without extensive fine-tuning. An alternative is latent based methods that are higher in efficiency and flexibility, but have lower general performance. This work is latent-based, and saught to improve the general performance and domain adaptation without adding to the costs and changing flexibility. 

MLPM has low training costs, high inference efficiency, high flexibility, and even higher performance than some of hte high cost LLM based methods. The training begins offline with sparse accumulation of weights to extract hidden states from each layer to calculate class prototypes, this then is used in a fine-tuning live training application. 

Three steps are required to move from latent spaces to a safety score. Prototypes (NMC) are the empirical means of latent representations for the safe and unsafe classes. NMC generalizes well ans is highly data-efficient. Mahalanobis distance is better to use than euclidean distance because it accounts for the variance and correlations of the latent space. Finally, probability is calculated via GDA, using a covariance matrix shared across classes that maps distances to a safety score, without high memory requirements. 

An important result is that there is no single layer that is the best for every model, and safety information is distributed across intermediate layers, meaning ambiguous inputs at a single layer are resolved by looking holisitically across the model architecture. This raises the necessity of fetermining which layer to use, and is done in the paper by using sparse L1 weights to pick the useful layers of the network. 

Experimental setup involved LLama, Mistral, OLMo and Qwen3. as well as 8 harmefullness and jailbreak datasets. This method beat more of the latent baselines, as well as the average performance for the guard models. The scalability was tested by running it on different sizes of active parameter models, and generally all models have better harmfulness detection on larger active parameter models. 
- I think an interesting future area of research is in improving the harmfulness detection on smaller models, it is necessary if we are going to use smaller models for things like European AI soverignty and the environmental impact of AI. 

The efficiency of MLPM has a miniscule computational overhead compared to other methods that can be 1000x or more costly. Future methods could try to optimize this further, as the implementation done in the opencodebase is not fully optimized. MLPM is able to be so fast due to the fact that it automatically optimizes the subset of model layers, since some models like mistral have middle layers being very important, whereas for LLama often the later layers are more improtant for safety information. 

Something that is less commonly compared in this area of research is the false refusal rate, and the MLPM with simple refusal ("I cannot help with this") had the best performance over activation steering or prompt steering. Other investigations done in the paper was on misses, to see if there was a commonality into when the model was failing to identify safe versus unsafe prompts. In the data that was analyzed one of the most common was social stereotypes and unfair discrminisation as well as sexual content, which in the dataset can have more ambiguous definitions of safe and unsafe replies. 

The main limitations of MLPM is the dependency on LLMs, so the models will need good representations on the input and reasoning steps to adequately judge safety. The method is also a system role, designes as a fast first line of defense that should be a part of a combination of multiple different methods to ensure the highest safety possible.
