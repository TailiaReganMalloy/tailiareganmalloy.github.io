Most of the blog posts on my personal site for the last year or so have been the notes that I have taken while attending a conference or an invited talk. This has been helpful for motivating me to take good notes of the presentations that I am interested in, and made it easy to post a monthly update on the research that I have been thinking of. However, outside of talks and presentations I have not been working on original research as much as I had been at other times in my career. There is a natural ebb and flow to the amount of time that one has to dedicate to research specifically. Sometimes it feels like I have a million different ideas for potential projects, but none of them are progressed enough for me to do concrete work on them, so I continue to think about new ideas and read papers in the meantime. At other points in my research career, particularly when finishing my thesis, it has felt like I have one single thing on my mind all of the time, making it difficult to balance work and my life outside of work. 

The past few months of my postdoc have been more simimlar to thesis writing compared to the feeling of entering a lab for the first time and hearing about all of the projects you would love to get involved in. I had been writing three grants simultaneously, and will continue to be writing and editing them for a short time longer, which has gotten me back into the mindset of thesis writing when I couldn't help but to focus on this singular thing most of the time. I have found that cooking and playing video games, more active things than watching movies or reading but not as more social options, has been a good balance for taking my mind off of work for a short while. 

Overall, my life as a postdoc has been much more balanced than when I was a PhD student. I think a big problem of that time was that I was managing my own time nearly completely, besides the few meetings that I would have with IBM or my advisor and any conferences or deadlines that needed to be met. This was during Covid lockdowns and there were very few reasons to need to go to campus, and only short conference meetings to break up the manotony of the day. Outside of those, I could chose to work 18 hour days two days a week and totally burn myself out if I chose to, which leads to issues with procrastination and avoidance that I think a lot of people who are managing their own time for the first time deal with. 

While writing grants can be stressful, I think overall my experience has been postivie, as looking forward to new research directions is less stressful for me than completing a piece of research that I have already done, which feels more defensive and personal when issues arrise. I hope that this experience with grant writing can be useful for informing my future self on how best to balance time and take my time off of work when I need to relax. Research can be natrually stressful, and I think getting experience like this makes it a lot easier to deal with the same processes in the future.
